01

VETRA: autonomous drone interception

Co-founder · since June 2026 · Basel

  • Jetson Orin Nano
  • Pixhawk
  • ROS2
  • Object tracking

Small, fast quadcopters are a growing risk for infrastructure and public events. Most existing counter-drone systems are expensive, need a lot of staff and are installed in one place. With VETRA we are working on a mobile interceptor that flies to a target drone on its own, catches it with a net and carries it out of the danger zone. I started VETRA together with a friend.

Technical implementation

  • Integrated perception and flight control on real hardware: Jetson Orin Nano, Pixhawk flight controller and an RGB camera.
  • Implemented autonomous flight, object tracking and a closed-loop approach towards a tracked target.
  • Currently: RL-based path planning, the net mechanism and the first functional prototype.

Progress

  1. Autonomous flight
  2. Object tracking
  3. Autonomous flight towards the tracked object
  4. Intercept flight
  5. Net launch with 360° shot
  6. Carry the target out of the danger zone

02

Real-World-Robotics: data collection for a dexterous robotic hand

Student · ongoing since September 2026 · Module run by the Soft Robotics Lab (SRL), ETH Zürich

  • NVIDIA Isaac Sim / Lab
  • Reinforcement learning
  • Imitation learning
  • Tactile sensing
  • Mechatronics
  • Prototyping

Real-World-Robotics is a project module at ETH Zürich, run by the Soft Robotics Lab. The project is ongoing and of great interest to me, because it brings robotic hardware, sensing and learning together on a real system.

What it is

We are developing a Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) for a 17-DoF tendon-driven robotic hand. It records joint and tactile sensor data 1:1 with the hand, together with its global position, while manipulation tasks are performed.

Our goal

Use this data to train reinforcement- and imitation-learning policies for dexterous manipulation, developed in NVIDIA Isaac Sim and Isaac Lab and tested on the real hand.

Digital training signatures at Endress+Hauser

Working student · 2023 – 2026 · Baselland

2'000+users across 3 production plants

Before

  2. Printed
  3. Signed on paper
  4. Filed in a closet
After
  1. Form generated automatically
  2. Worker data filled in
  3. Signed at the machine
  4. Tracked and reminded

The problem

Every production machine requires a training signature from the people who operate it. The forms were written by hand in Word, printed, signed and stored in a closet: thousands of documents, no overview and no reminders when a training was due.

What I built

A Python and C++ application that generates the documents automatically. The right form opens with the worker's data already filled in, it is signed digitally at the machine, and missing signatures are tracked and followed up.

Result

The paper workflow is gone end to end. Records are audit-proof and can be found in seconds. The tool is live in three plants and used daily.

04

Bachelor thesis: ML micro-macro relations for random fibre networks

Grade 6.0 / 6.0 · ETH Zürich, Institute for Mechanical Systems (Mazza group) · 2026

  • Residual NNs
  • PCE / Sobol'
  • Continuum mechanics

Challenge

A recent continuum theory for non-affine fibre networks[1] needs fibre-stretch distributions as input, which normally come from costly discrete simulations. I explored whether machine learning can predict fibre behaviour from local geometry instead.

Method

  • Sensitivity analysis with polynomial chaos expansion (PCE) and Sobol' indices.
  • Deep residual neural networks trained with a distribution-aware Sinkhorn loss.

Results

  • Local information is enough for accurate fibre-stretch prediction.
  • Most cases below 10 % energy error, some below 2 %.
  • Shows the potential of ML for using the continuum theory in practice.

Supervision and support: Prof. Edoardo Mazza, Dr. Alex Ehret, Dr. Ehsan Hosseini, Sara Mauro, Haotian Xu.

[1] Britt, B. R., & Ehret, A. E. (2022). Constitutive modelling of fibre networks with stretch distributions. Part I: Theory and illustration. Journal of the Mechanics and Physics of Solids, 167, 104960. doi:10.1016/j.jmps.2022.104960

CeraSeal: surgical technology for C-section repair

ETH Focus Project · grade 6.0 / 6.0 · 9-person team · Sep 2025 – Jun 2026

  • Machine design
  • Mechatronics
  • Strain analysis
  • Medical devices

An interdisciplinary student team developing a PLA/PCL patch that reinforces the closure of the uterus after a C-section and supports healing. The work covered patch manufacturing, mechanical testing, a surgical simulation platform and ultrasound sensing.

My contributions

  • Designed and built a custom calendering machine for controlled patch manufacturing.
  • Developed tracking and strain-mapping analysis to quantify stress concentrations at the sutures and the reinforcement from the patch.

Outcome

  • Working patch prototypes combining mechanical reinforcement, sealing and ultrasound visibility.
  • Strain analysis software that tracks markers and evaluates how the patch integrates mechanically with the tissue.

Partners: University of Zurich, University Hospital Zurich, Balgrist Spinal Cord Injury Center, Zurich University of the Arts. There is no public project website because of an NDA; the IP is owned by ZüriMed AG.
